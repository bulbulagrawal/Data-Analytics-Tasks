# Day-1
1. What is Data?
Data is a collection of recorded facts, observations, measurements, or events. It can describe
customers.

2. What is Data Analytics?
Data Analytics is the process of examining data to answer questions, identify patterns, understand problems, and support decisions.
Companies generate data every day. Analytics helps them turn that data into useful business knowledge.

3. Data Analyst role or What does a Data Analyst do

A Data Analyst helps an organization answer business questions using data.

Typical responsibilities include:

- Understanding business requirements.
- Finding and collecting relevant data.
- Checking data accuracy and completeness.
- Cleaning and organizing data.
- Analyzing data using spreadsheets, SQL, or programming tools.
- Creating charts, reports, and dashboards.
- Explaining findings to stakeholders.
- Recommending actions based on evidence.

The analyst's workflow

1. Understand the business question
2. Collect relevant data
3. Validate and clean the data
4. Analyze and compare
5. Visualize the results
6. Explain findings and recommend action
7. Measure the outcome

4. Data Analyst vs. Data Scientist vs. Data Engineer
   These roles work together, but they generally focus on different problems.
   Data Analyst Focus: What happened, why might it have happened, and what should we do?
   Data Scientist Focus: Build statistical models and predictive or experimental solutions.
   Data Engineer Focus: Build and maintain reliable systems that collect, transform, and deliver data.

A simple way to remember the distinction:
  - Analyst: Understands and explains business data.
  - Scientist: Develops models to estimate, predict, or test.
  - Engineer: Builds the systems that make data available and reliable.

5. Types of data
Data can be classified in several ways. Two important classifications are its structure and the type of values it contains.
  A. Structured data
     Structured data follows a defined format, often rows and columns.
  B. Unstructured data
     Unstructured data does not naturally follow a fixed table format.
     Examples:
      - Product review text.
      - Photographs.
      - Videos.
      - Audio recordings.
      - Emails and documents.
  C. Semi-structured data
     Semi-structured data has labels, keys, or markers, but does not necessarily follow a fixed table schema.
  D. Qualitative vs. quantitative data
     Qualitative data describes categories or qualities.
     Quantitative data represents numerical quantities.
     
     Qualitative data : Usually describes categories or characteristics.
     Examples: product color, customer feedback category, city, payment method.
     
     Quantitative data : Represents amounts or counts.
     Examples: revenue, age, order quantity, delivery time.

     Quantitative data can be further divided into:
      - Discrete: Countable values, such as the number of orders.
      - Continuous: Measurements that can take a range of values, such as delivery time or package weight.
    
     One important detail: a number is not always quantitative data. A customer ID such as 101 is an identifier, not a measured quantity.           Adding customer IDs together would generally have no useful meaning.

6. Data sources : A data source is the place or system from which data originates.
   A Data Analyst collects information from multiple sources depending on the business problem.

- Databases: Store organized records that can be queried using SQL. Examples: MySQL, PostgreSQL, and SQL Server.
- Excel Files: Store tabular data for calculations, analysis, and reporting.
- CSV Files: Store tabular information in a plain-text, comma-separated format.
- APIs: Allow applications to exchange data programmatically.
- Websites: Provide publicly available information that may be collected through permitted web scraping.
- Surveys and Forms: Collect customer opinions, satisfaction ratings, and demographic information.
- Business Applications: Provide information from CRM, ERP, HR, finance, and sales systems.
- Logs and Sensors: Record application activity, system events, and measurements from connected devices.

This is a simplified workflow. In real projects, analysts often return to earlier steps when they discover missing data or realize that the original question needs clarification.

7. Data Analyst Workflow
    A Data Analyst follows a systematic process to transform raw data into meaningful insights that help businesses make informed decisions.

Workflow Summary

1. Define the Business Problem :Understand the objective, identify the problem, and determine the questions that need answers.
2. Collect Data : Gather relevant data from spreadsheets, databases, surveys, APIs, and business applications.
3. Clean and Prepare Data : Handle missing values, remove duplicates, correct errors, and standardize formats.
4. Analyze Data : Use calculations, comparisons, statistics, and patterns to answer business questions.
5. Visualize Results : Present findings using charts, tables, reports, and dashboards.
6. Communicate Insights : Explain what the data reveals, why it matters, and what action should be taken.
7. Support Decisions and Monitor Outcomes : Recommend actions, track results, and refine the analysis when new questions arise.

# Workflow in One Line

Business Problem → Data Collection → Data Cleaning → Data Analysis → Data Visualization → Communication → Decision-Making

Note: The workflow is iterative. Analysts may return to earlier steps when they discover data-quality issues or need to investigate new questions.

8. Key Takeaways :
- Raw data contains facts and figures that become meaningful when organized and interpreted in context.
- Data Analytics helps identify trends, patterns, and insights that guide better business decisions.
- A Data Analyst turns data into useful information by collecting, preparing, examining, and presenting it.
- Data can be categorized by its characteristics, including qualitative and quantitative data, as well as by how it is organized, such as   structured and unstructured data.
- Organizations obtain data from various sources, including databases, spreadsheets, customer feedback, APIs, and business software.
- Reliable analysis depends on accurate data, suitable analytical methods, and clear explanations of the findings.
- Understanding the business question is essential for ensuring that analysis delivers useful and relevant results.
- The purpose of data analysis is to turn findings into useful insights that help solve business problems and improve outcomes.
